## 1. Core frameworks and what they showed

### Takeaway
Between 2023 and 2024 the field settled on four families of architecture. **Role-play/SOP pipelines** (CAMEL, MetaGPT, ChatDev, AgentVerse) give agents fixed roles. **Debate/discussion** (Du et al., Liang et al. MAD) has agents argue toward an answer. **Orchestrator-worker** (AutoGen, Magentic-One, Anthropic Research) uses a lead agent that delegates to workers. **Graph/layered ensembles** (GPTSwarm, Mixture-of-Agents) wire agents into an optimizable network. The early papers report gains mostly against weak single-call baselines, with no compute matching. Their lasting contributions are the structural ideas: SOPs and structured artifacts, ledgers for orchestration, and independent proposals followed by aggregation.

### Cited Findings
**Role-based / SOP**
- **CAMEL** (Li et al., Mar 2023, arXiv 2303.17760). "Role-playing" between an AI-user agent and an AI-assistant agent, steered by "inception prompting" so that little human input is needed. It was used to generate the AI Society and Code conversation datasets. Documented failure modes: role flipping, assistant repeating instructions, flake replies, and infinite conversation loops. These are early versions of MAST's "disobey role spec" and "step repetition" categories. [abstract-level, not re-fetched] — [alphaXiv 2303.17760](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2303.17760)
- **Generative Agents** (Park et al., Apr 2023, arXiv 2304.03442; UIST 2023). 25 agents live in a sandbox town ("Smallville"). The architecture has three parts: a memory stream (a natural-language log retrieved by recency, importance, and relevance), reflection (higher-level syntheses of memories), and planning. Emergent behavior: one seeded intention to throw a Valentine's Day party spread through the town by invitation. Ablations showed that observation, planning, and reflection each contribute to believability. This is the canonical *shared/long-term memory* design. [abstract-level, not re-fetched] — [alphaXiv 2304.03442](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2304.03442)
- **MetaGPT** (Hong et al., Aug 2023, arXiv 2308.00352; ICLR 2024). Human Standard Operating Procedures are encoded into an assembly line of roles (PM, Architect, Engineer, QA). Agents exchange **structured documents** (PRDs, design docs) rather than free chat, through a publish–subscribe shared message pool, with executable feedback. Reported 85.9% (HumanEval) and 87.7% (MBPP) Pass@1. [abstract-level, not re-fetched] — [alphaXiv 2308.00352](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2308.00352)
- **ChatDev** (Qian et al., Jul 2023, arXiv 2307.07924; ACL 2024). A virtual software company follows waterfall phases (design → coding → testing → documentation) through a "chat chain" of two-agent dialogues. It uses "communicative dehallucination", where an agent asks for specifics before answering. [abstract-level, not re-fetched] — [alphaXiv 2307.07924](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2307.07924)
- MAST's cross-system comparison (GPT-4o): **MetaGPT had 60–68% fewer system-design (FC1) and inter-agent-misalignment (FC2) failures than ChatDev, but 1.56× more verification (FC3) failures**. The authors attribute this to MetaGPT's SOP adherence versus ChatDev's explicit test/review phases. — [Cemri et al., arXiv 2503.13657](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2503.13657)
- **AgentVerse** (Chen et al., Aug 2023, arXiv 2308.10848; ICLR 2024). A four-stage loop: expert recruitment (dynamic role generation), collaborative decision-making (horizontal/democratic or vertical/solver-reviewer structures), action execution, and evaluation, which feeds back into recruitment. Reported emergent behaviors include volunteering, conformity, and destructive behaviors. [abstract-level, not re-fetched] — [alphaXiv 2308.10848](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2308.10848)

**Debate**
- **Du et al., "Improving Factuality and Reasoning in Language Models through Multiagent Debate"** (May 2023, arXiv 2305.14325; ICML 2024). Several instances (typically 3 agents × 2 rounds) propose answers, read each other's answers, and update them. Reported gains over single-agent and reflection on arithmetic, GSM8K, chess-move prediction, and biography factuality. It used the same model for all agents and compared against single-sample baselines, not compute-matched self-consistency. [abstract-level, not re-fetched] — [alphaXiv 2305.14325](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2305.14325)
- **Liang et al., "Encouraging Divergent Thinking … through Multi-Agent Debate" (MAD)** (May 2023, arXiv 2305.19118; EMNLP 2024). Identifies **Degeneration-of-Thought**: once a model is confident, self-reflection cannot produce new ideas. Proposes an affirmative debater, a negative debater, and a judge. Evaluated on commonsense machine translation and Counter-Intuitive Arithmetic Reasoning (CIAR). Found that a *moderate* level of "tit-for-tat" disagreement works best and that an adaptive stopping judge helps. [abstract-level, not re-fetched] — [alphaXiv 2305.19118](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2305.19118)

**Orchestrator-worker / conversation programming**
- **AutoGen** (Wu et al., Aug 2023, arXiv 2308.08155). Introduced "conversable agents" (LLM-, tool-, or human-backed) and "conversation programming", where control flow is expressed as multi-agent chat (two-agent chats, group chat with a manager). Demonstrated on math, retrieval-augmented QA, ALFWorld decision-making, and coding with a safeguard agent. [abstract-level, not re-fetched] — [alphaXiv 2308.08155](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2308.08155)
- **Magentic-One** (Fourney et al., Microsoft, Nov 2024, arXiv 2411.04468). A lead **Orchestrator** maintains a **Task Ledger** (facts, guesses, plan) in an outer loop and a **Progress Ledger** (is the task done, is progress being made, who acts next) in an inner loop. When progress stalls it replans. Four workers: WebSurfer, FileSurfer, Coder, ComputerTerminal. It reached performance "statistically competitive" with the state of the art on GAIA, AssistantBench, and WebArena without task-specific changes. It also shipped AutoGenBench. [abstract-level, not re-fetched] — [alphaXiv 2411.04468](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2411.04468). In MAST's later testing, Magentic-One served as an *out-of-domain* system for validating the taxonomy (κ = 0.79) — [arXiv 2503.13657](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2503.13657)

**Graph / layered**
- **GPTSwarm, "Language Agents as Optimizable Graphs"** (Zhuge et al., Feb 2024, arXiv 2402.16823; ICML 2024). Agents are computational graphs whose nodes are LLM or tool operations. It optimizes node prompts and edges (connectivity, via REINFORCE over edge probabilities), so that multi-agent topology becomes a learnable object. [abstract-level, not re-fetched] — [alphaXiv 2402.16823](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2402.16823)
- **Mixture-of-Agents (MoA)** (Wang et al., Together AI, Jun 2024, arXiv 2406.04692; ICLR 2025). Layers of proposer LLMs each see all outputs from the previous layer; a final aggregator synthesizes. The paper names the effect "collaborativeness": LLMs write better answers when shown other models' answers, even weaker ones. Reported **65.1% on AlpacaEval 2.0 (LC win rate) using only open-source models, vs 57.5% for GPT-4o**. [abstract-level, not re-fetched] — [alphaXiv 2406.04692](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2406.04692)
- In a 2026 head-to-head on 11 verifier-scored optimization tasks, MoA was the **only** multi-agent configuration with positive interaction gain for both same-model (+0.012) and diverse-model (+0.016) teams. It was also the only one where proposers never saw each other's outputs. — [Ann, Liu, Tan, "The Interaction Tax", arXiv 2608.23541](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.23541)

### Inferences
- The frameworks that hold up best in later critiques share one feature: **independent generation followed by structured aggregation or verification** (MoA, self-consistency-like motifs, SOP documents, executable tests). Free-form multi-turn chat between peers tends to hold up worst.
- MetaGPT versus ChatDev suggests that **structure (SOPs, typed artifacts) and verification (testing phases) are separate levers**. A harness needs both.

### Gaps
- I did not re-fetch the original abstracts of CAMEL, Generative Agents, AutoGen, MetaGPT, ChatDev, AgentVerse, GPTSwarm, MoA, Magentic-One, or Du/Liang, because arxiv.org was blocked and the tool budget was limited. Their numbers above should be verified against the abstracts.
- None of the original papers compared against compute-matched single-agent baselines. This is the main reason for the critiques in Section 2.

## 2. Empirical critiques and failure analyses

### Takeaway
Two bodies of evidence point the same way. Under **equal compute and strong prompting**, single agents with self-consistency (CoT-SC) usually match or beat debate and automatically generated MAS. When MAS fail, the cause is mostly **system design and coordination (~76% of failure annotations)** rather than base-model weakness, and verification remains a persistent weak point. Multi-agent gains appear only in specific regimes: no demonstrations or knowledge available, diverse models with independent proposals, locatable faults that critique can fix, or tasks that are truly parallelizable or exceed one context window, run with frontier models and expert-designed structure.

### Cited Findings
**MAST: "Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail?"** (Cemri, Pan, Yang, … Zaharia, Gonzalez, Stoica; UC Berkeley; Mar 2025, arXiv 2503.13657; NeurIPS 2025 Datasets & Benchmarks)
- Method: grounded-theory analysis of 150 traces from 5 frameworks (HyperAgent, AppWorld, AG2, ChatDev, MetaGPT) by 6 experts. Inter-annotator κ = 0.88. An o1-based LLM-as-judge annotator reached 94% accuracy and κ = 0.77 against humans. Out-of-domain validation on OpenManus, Magentic-One, MMLU, and GAIA gave κ = 0.79. The final MAST-Data release contains **1,642 traces across 7 MAS frameworks** and 4 model families (GPT-4, Claude 3, Qwen2.5, CodeLlama). — [arXiv 2503.13657](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2503.13657)
- State-of-the-art open MAS failure rates in their analysis: **41% to 86.7%**. — [arXiv 2503.13657](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2503.13657)
- **14 failure modes in 3 categories** (prevalence = share of failure annotations):
  - **FC1 System Design Issues, 44.2%**: FM-1.1 Disobey task specification 11.8%; FM-1.2 Disobey role specification 1.5%; **FM-1.3 Step repetition 15.7%**; FM-1.4 Loss of conversation history 2.8%; **FM-1.5 Unaware of termination conditions 12.4%**.
  - **FC2 Inter-Agent Misalignment, 32.3%**: FM-2.1 Conversation reset 2.2%; FM-2.2 Fail to ask for clarification 6.8%; FM-2.3 Task derailment 7.4%; FM-2.4 Information withholding 0.8%; FM-2.5 Ignored other agent's input 1.9%; **FM-2.6 Reasoning–action mismatch 13.2%**.
  - **FC3 Task Verification, 23.5%**: FM-3.1 Premature termination 6.2%; FM-3.2 No or incomplete verification 8.2%; FM-3.3 Incorrect verification 9.1%.
  — [arXiv 2503.13657](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2503.13657)
- Correlations between the three categories are low (0.17–0.32), so they measure distinct things. FM-1.5 (termination unawareness) and FM-2.4 (information withholding) are "fatal": they appear almost only in failed runs. FM-3.2 and FM-3.3 also appear in *successful* runs, which points to latent weakness. — [arXiv 2503.13657](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2503.13657)
- Model comparison in MetaGPT: GPT-4o had 39% fewer FC1 failures than Claude 3.7 Sonnet, but **both struggled with FC3 verification**. — [arXiv 2503.13657](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2503.13657)
- Interventions (better role prompts, topology changes) gave **+9.4% to +15.6%** success for ChatDev on ProgramDev with the same LLM. The authors judged these gains "limited" and concluded that fundamental redesign is needed. The paper's insights: simple communication protocols do not fix FC2, which needs "social reasoning" or theory-of-mind about what other agents need to know; final-stage, low-level checks alone are insufficient, so verification should happen at multiple levels. — [arXiv 2503.13657](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2503.13657)

**"Rethinking the Bounds of LLM Reasoning: Are Multi-Agent Discussions the Key?"** (Wang et al., Feb 2024, arXiv 2402.18272; ACL 2024)
- Evaluated on ECQA, GSM8K, and FOLIO-wiki with ChatGPT-3.5, Gemini Pro, and Bard (temperature 0.25, ≤3 rounds). Compared Debate, MAD, ReConcile, and the authors' Conquer-and-Merge Discussion (CMD). — [arXiv 2402.18272](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2402.18272)
- **A single agent with strong prompts (detailed question description + answer format + task-specific demonstration) performs comparably to the best multi-agent discussion.** Multi-agent discussion beats a single agent **only when no demonstrations are given**. With mixed models, weaker LLMs improve by discussing with stronger ones. — [arXiv 2402.18272](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2402.18272)
- Error types observed: **judge mistakes** (the judge picks an inferior answer even though a correct one is present) and **wrong-answer propagation** (an incorrect answer gains support until it becomes consensus). — [arXiv 2402.18272](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2402.18272)

**"Should we be going MAD? A Look at Multi-Agent Debate Strategies for LLMs"** (Smit, Grinsztajn, Duckworth, Barrett, Pretorius; InstaDeep; Nov 2023, arXiv 2311.17371; ICML 2024)
- Benchmarked Society of Minds (Du et al.), Multi-Persona (Liang et al.), and ChatEval against single-agent prompting, Self-Consistency, Ensemble Refinement, and Medprompt. Seven datasets: MedQA, PubMedQA, MMLU-clinical, CosmosQA, CIAR, GPQA, Chess. Mostly GPT-3.5-turbo, with some GPT-4 and Mixtral 8x7B. — [arXiv 2311.17371](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2311.17371)
- **MAD "did not reliably outperform" self-consistency or ensembling. Medprompt (no debate) was often best and cheaper.** No protocol dominated across datasets. MAD is **highly sensitive to hyperparameters** (agents, rounds, summarization, agent prompts), and optimal settings did not carry over to held-out datasets in k-fold tests. — [arXiv 2311.17371](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2311.17371)
- Multi-Persona (the angel/devil design) often made the *first agent's answer worse*, because the devil argues against even correct answers. **"Agreement modulation"** (prompting agents to agree X% of the time) improved Multi-Persona on MedQA by about 15%, enough to beat Medprompt and SC as the best GPT-3.5 result. The optimal agreement level depends on the dataset: high for MedQA and PubMedQA, low for the counter-intuitive CIAR. Tuned settings carried over to GPT-4 but not to Mixtral. — [arXiv 2311.17371](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2311.17371)

**"More Agents Is All You Need"** (Li et al., Tencent, Feb 2024, arXiv 2402.05120; TMLR 2024)
- Plain **sampling-and-voting**: performance rises with the number of instantiated agents (samples), and the method is orthogonal to and stackable with CoT, debate, and similar methods. The gain grows with task difficulty. Reported that a small open model ensembled (e.g., Llama2-13B) can match a much larger one (Llama2-70B) on some tasks. [abstract-level, not re-fetched] — [alphaXiv 2402.05120](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2402.05120). (Agent-count scaling belongs to the other researcher's scope and is noted here only briefly.)

**Later debate critiques, 2025–2026**
- *Stop Overvaluing Multi-Agent Debate* (Zhang et al., Feb 2025, arXiv 2502.08788): a systematic evaluation of several MAD methods across many benchmarks and models found that MAD often fails to beat CoT and self-consistency, and that **model heterogeneity** is the more promising lever. [abstract-level, not re-fetched] — [alphaXiv 2502.08788](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2502.08788)
- *Debate or Vote* (Choi et al., Aug 2025, arXiv 2508.17536; NeurIPS 2025): split MAD into voting and inter-agent debate and found that **majority voting accounts for most of the gains**. They model debate alone as a martingale over agents' beliefs, so in expectation it does not improve correctness. [abstract-level, not re-fetched] — [alphaXiv 2508.17536](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2508.17536)
- *The Cost of Consensus: Isolated Self-Correction Prevails Over Unguided Homogeneous Multi-Agent Debate* (Apr 2026, arXiv 2605.00914). The title states the finding; I did not read the details. — [alphaXiv 2605.00914](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.00914)
- *The Consistency Illusion* (Jun 2026, arXiv 2606.08457), on medical QA: answer-level consensus in debate can hide misaligned reasoning, so **agreement is not a reliability signal**. Abstract only. — [alphaXiv 2606.08457](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.08457)
- Other 2026 debate-dynamics papers, read only by title or abstract: *What Does Multi-Agent Debate Actually Change?* (CMU, Sep 2026, arXiv 2609.08016); *Not All Flips Are Conformity* (May 2026, arXiv 2606.00820); *One Axis, No Brake: Self-Knowledge Limits the Filtering of Harmful Peer Conformity* (Jul 2026, arXiv 2609.18998); *Misinformation Propagation in Benign Multi-Agent Systems* (Jun 2026, arXiv 2606.16710). Counter-trend papers claiming debate can be rescued: *Beyond Solo and Consistency: Vindicating MAD via Conditional Progressive Pruning* (Sep 2026, arXiv 2609.33974) and *Confidence-Guided Adaptive Debate* (Oct 2026, arXiv 2610.16343). — [2609.08016](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.08016), [2606.00820](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.00820), [2609.18998](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.18998), [2606.16710](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.16710), [2609.33974](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.33974), [2610.16343](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.16343)

**Equal-budget single-agent vs MAS, 2026**
- **Tran & Kiela (Stanford), "Single-Agent LLMs Outperform Multi-Agent Systems on Multi-Hop Reasoning Under Equal Thinking Token Budgets"** (Apr 2026, arXiv 2604.02460). Thinking-token budgets were fixed at 100–10,000 tokens. Sequential, parallel-roles, debate, and ensemble MAS were compared against a single agent on FRAMES and 4-hop MuSiQue, using Qwen3, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama, and Gemini 2.5. Reported result: the single agent matches or beats MAS. Caveats: budget control for Gemini is approximate, and Sanjeev Arora publicly questioned the main table. I relied on secondary summaries and did not read the paper. — [arXiv 2604.02460 (via search)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02460); [beancount.io summary](https://beancount.io/bean-labs/research-logs/2026/05/31/single-agent-outperforms-multi-agent-equal-token-budget)
- **"The Illusion of Multi-Agent Advantage"** (Jwalapuram, … Joty; Salesforce Research et al.; Jun 2026, arXiv 2606.13003). Six automatic MAS (DyLAN, MAS-Zero, ADAS, AFlow, MaAS, MAS-Orchestra) were compared with **CoT-SC (5-sample vote)** on GPQA-Diamond, HLE-Maths, SWE-Bench Lite, and BrowseComp-Plus, using GPT-4o, GPT-5, GPT-OSS-120B, and Gemini-2.5-Pro. — [arXiv 2606.13003](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.13003)
  - Automatic MAS **consistently underperformed CoT-SC**, which often matched or exceeded them at **<10% of their cost**. A single GPT-5 with CoT-SC beat GPT-4o-based ADAS and AFlow using **less than half the tokens**, so orchestration did not close a gap between model generations. A significant MAS uplift appeared only on HLE-Math, and only with frontier backbones (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro). The authors call this a "competency floor". — [arXiv 2606.13003](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.13003)
  - The authors built SMFR, a 588-item synthetic multi-hop financial benchmark designed around Anthropic's criteria for MAS-suitable tasks: decomposable, needle-in-haystack context, parallelizable. Automatic MAS still lost to CoT-SC there. A **hand-designed "Expert-MAS"** (code-driven Python orchestrator dispatching Extractor and Calculator agents in parallel per investor, with deterministic final comparison) raised **GPT-5 from 57.0% (CoT-SC) to 96.5%** and GPT-OSS from 26.1% to 36.1%, at cost comparable to CoT-SC. GPT-4o was bottlenecked by its base calculation and retrieval ability even with the expert design. — [arXiv 2606.13003](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.13003)
  - Inside the automatic MAS they found pathologies. DyLAN's role agents reached immediate unanimous consensus in about 70% of cases (GPT-4o) and over 90% (GPT-5), and an "all-assistant" setup beat the specialized roles. MAS-Zero's verifier showed positional bias toward early context entries (>45% with GPT-4o), making later workers "expensive witnesses". ADAS's positive signal came from self-consistency motifs, while specialized coordination motifs added negligible gains. **50% of AFlow workflows collapsed to "run one prompt 3× then aggregate"**, which amounts to CoT-SC. MaAS showed "cost-minimizing collapse", with 74.2% trivial I/O calls on BrowseComp-Plus. MAS-Orchestra defaulted to Debate and Reflexion regardless of difficulty. — [arXiv 2606.13003](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.13003)
- **"The Interaction Tax"** (Ann, Liu, Tan; U. Chicago; Aug 2026, arXiv 2608.23541). Setup: 11 verifier-scored optimization tasks (several from the AlphaEvolve suite); Claude Sonnet 4, GPT-4o, and Gemini 2.5 Flash; identical budgets (200K tokens, 600 s). — [arXiv 2608.23541](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.23541)
  - Across 10 configurations, **none reliably beat the best single-agent baseline** (Best-of-N, Self-Refine, or verifier-guided evolutionary search). — [arXiv 2608.23541](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.23541)
  - Model diversity helps: a 2×2 factorial design gave a diversity coefficient of +0.188 (95% CI [0.073, 0.299]). Every same-model team scored zero on at least one task, while the diverse team never did. **Full-solution exchange erases this diversity**: interaction gain turned negative for diverse teams (Chain −0.024, MAgICoRe −0.035, Debate −0.078), and mean pairwise solution distance fell from 0.315 to 0.229 after **a single round**. Synthesizers copied the best proposal at least 80% of the time on 5 of 7 tasks. — [arXiv 2608.23541](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.23541)
  - Critique helps when faults are **locatable and repairable**: on Knapsack capacity violations, diverse Debate reached 10/10 feasible versus 2/10 for same-model Debate. It hurts when faults are hard to find: on 3AP-Free-100, diverse Debate got 0/10 versus 6/10. On graded tasks, the first critique round **degraded** solutions in 57% of runs. — [arXiv 2608.23541](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.23541)

### Inferences
- The baseline to beat is **CoT-SC or best-of-N with a strong prompt at matched tokens**, not a single call. Any multi-agent component in the harness should be A/B tested against this baseline.
- The debate literature agrees on a few points. Voting or aggregation does most of the work. Exchanging peers' full solutions causes conformity and collapses diversity. Agreement among agents is not evidence of correctness. Critique pays off only when an external check or a crisp error signal is available.
- The positive cases have structure in common: **expert-designed decomposition, deterministic code orchestration, parallel independent workers, and deterministic aggregation**. In Illusion's SMFR, this took GPT-5 from 57% to 96.5%. Automatic MAS search did not find such designs.

### Gaps
- MAST's prevalence percentages are shares of failure annotations from the LLM annotator over MAST-Data. They are not per-trace incidence rates. The report should not describe them as "X% of runs".
- I could not read Tran & Kiela in full, and the result is publicly disputed.
- Recent 2026 debate papers were reviewed only by title or abstract.

## 3. Practitioner evidence (Anthropic, Cognition)

### Takeaway
Anthropic's production Research system is an orchestrator-worker design. It beats a single agent by 90.2% on breadth-first research, mainly because it **spends more tokens in parallel across separate context windows** (token usage explains 80% of variance) at about 15× chat cost. Anthropic and Cognition agree that tightly coupled tasks such as coding are poorly served by parallel agents that do not share full context.

### Cited Findings
**Anthropic, "How we built our multi-agent research system"** (Jun 13, 2025)
- Setup: Claude Opus 4 as lead agent, Claude Sonnet 4 as subagents. It **outperformed single-agent Opus 4 by 90.2%** on Anthropic's internal research eval. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)
- On BrowseComp, three factors explained **95% of performance variance**, and **token usage alone explained 80%**. The other two were number of tool calls and model choice. Upgrading to Sonnet 4 gave a larger gain than doubling the token budget on Sonnet 3.7. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)
- Cost: agents use **~4×** the tokens of chat, and multi-agent systems **~15×**. This is only worthwhile for high-value tasks. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)
- **Helps**: breadth-first queries, heavy parallelization, information that exceeds a single context window, many complex tools. **Hurts**: tasks where all agents need the same context or that have many inter-agent dependencies. "Most coding tasks" fall here, and agents are not yet good at real-time coordination. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)
- Parallelization (lead spawns 3–5 subagents in parallel; each subagent makes 3+ parallel tool calls) cut research time **by up to 90%** for complex queries. A tool-testing agent that rewrote tool descriptions reduced task completion time by **40%**. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)
- Effort-scaling rules are written into the prompts. Simple fact-finding: 1 agent, 3–10 tool calls. Comparisons: 2–4 subagents, 10–15 calls each. Complex research: more than 10 subagents. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)
- Observed failure modes: spawning **50 subagents for simple queries**; endless searches for sources that do not exist; excessive updates that distracted other agents; preferring SEO content farms. Vague delegation caused **duplicated work**. The fix was explicit delegation with objective, output format, tool/source guidance, and task boundaries. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)
- Memory and artifacts: the lead saves its plan to external memory, because contexts beyond 200K tokens are truncated. Subagents write outputs to external storage and pass back **lightweight references**, which avoids a "game of telephone" through the coordinator. Fresh subagents are spawned with clean contexts after phases are summarized. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)
- Evaluation: start with about 20 queries. Use a single LLM-judge prompt that outputs a 0.0–1.0 score plus pass/fail on factual accuracy, citation accuracy, completeness, source quality, and tool efficiency. Judge end states rather than paths, and keep human testing. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)
- Reliability and bottlenecks: errors compound in stateful agents, so the system resumes from checkpoints and uses retries. Full tracing and rainbow deployments are in place. **Synchronous execution** is a bottleneck: the lead waits for each batch, cannot steer subagents mid-run, and one slow subagent blocks everything. Asynchronous execution is planned. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)

**Anthropic, "Building effective agents"** (Schluntz & Zhang, Dec 19, 2024)
- Distinguishes **workflows** (LLMs and tools orchestrated along predefined code paths) from **agents** (the LLM directs its own process). Advises finding the simplest solution first and adding complexity only when it demonstrably helps. Patterns: prompt chaining, routing, parallelization (sectioning and voting), **orchestrator-workers**, and **evaluator-optimizer**. Principles: simplicity, transparency (show planning), and careful agent-computer interface/tool design. [content from my knowledge of the post; not re-fetched this session] — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)

**Cognition (Walden Yan), "Don't Build Multi-Agents"** (Jun 2025). cognition.ai was unreachable, so this is from secondary coverage.
- Principle 1: **share context**, meaning full agent traces rather than single messages. Principle 2: **actions carry implicit decisions**, so parallel agents make conflicting implicit choices. The often-cited example: one subagent builds a Flappy Bird background in a style that clashes with another subagent's bird. — [echai.ventures summary](https://echai.ventures/how-founders-use-ai/r/don-t-build-multi-agents--941); [Biweekly Engineering](https://biweekly-engineering.beehiiv.com/p/the-case-against-multi-agents-biweekly-engineering-episode-42)
- Recommendation: a **single-threaded linear agent**. For very long tasks, a dedicated LLM compresses history into key decisions and events (Cognition experimented with fine-tuned smaller models for this). Each handoff is a chance to lose context. — [yespress.io profile](https://yespress.io/walden-yan); [Biweekly Engineering](https://biweekly-engineering.beehiiv.com/p/the-case-against-multi-agents-biweekly-engineering-episode-42)
- A follow-up in Apr 2026 reportedly kept the objection to *parallel writer swarms* but described practical multi-agent flows Cognition has since built into Devin. Secondary sources only. — [latent.space wiki](https://latent.space/wiki/walden-yan-7c572c23/); [traeai.com](https://www.traeai.com/articles/f89070d0-6418-4e01-b0ae-d02b3fbb81f4)

### Inferences
- Anthropic's and Cognition's positions are compatible. Use multi-agent **read-heavy, parallel exploration** (research, search, review), where workers return compressed findings. Keep **write-heavy, coupled work** (code edits, a single artifact) in one thread or one writer.
- Anthropic's 80% token-variance finding and the Illusion/Tran-Kiela equal-budget results describe the same effect from two sides. Much of the multi-agent gain is **more total test-time compute**, delivered in a way that gets around per-context limits. When a single context can hold the work and budgets are matched, the advantage shrinks or disappears.

### Gaps
- I could not read Cognition's original essay or the 2026 follow-up directly, so exact quotes are unverified.
- Anthropic did not publish absolute scores or a compute-matched single-agent baseline for the 90.2% figure.

## 4. Recent 2025–2026 work: design principles, topology search, benchmarks

### Takeaway
Automated MAS design (ADAS → AFlow → MASS → MaAS, and dozens of 2026 follow-ups) reports gains against hand-designed baselines. Its two most transferable lessons are that **prompt optimization of individual agents matters more than topology**, and that **useful topologies are sparse and task-specific**. The 2026 audit "Illusion of Multi-Agent Advantage" shows that many searched topologies collapse to self-consistency in practice.

### Cited Findings
- **ADAS, Automated Design of Agentic Systems** (Hu, Lu, Clune; Aug 2024, arXiv 2408.08435; ICLR 2025). A "Meta Agent Search" agent writes new agents as code and builds on an archive. Reported F1 +13.6 on DROP and +14.4% on MGSM, with transfer across domains and models. [abstract-level, not re-fetched] — [alphaXiv 2408.08435](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2408.08435)
- **AFlow** (Zhang et al., Oct 2024, arXiv 2410.10762; ICLR 2025). Monte-Carlo tree search over code-represented workflows built from operators (Ensemble, Review, Revise, …). Reported a 5.7% average improvement over state-of-the-art baselines on 6 benchmarks, and smaller models beating GPT-4o on some tasks at 4.55% of its inference cost. [abstract-level, not re-fetched] — [alphaXiv 2410.10762](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2410.10762). Counter-evidence: in the 2026 audit, **50% of AFlow workflows degenerated to a single prompt run 3× and aggregated**. — [arXiv 2606.13003](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.13003)
- **MaAS, Multi-agent Architecture Search via Agentic Supernet** (Zhang et al., Feb 2025, arXiv 2502.04180; ICML 2025). Samples a query-dependent architecture from a probabilistic supernet and reports a fraction of the inference cost of prior MAS (roughly 6–45%) with modest accuracy gains. [abstract-level, not re-fetched; verify exact ranges] — [alphaXiv 2502.04180](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2502.04180). In the 2026 audit, MaAS showed "cost-minimizing collapse" (74.2% trivial I/O calls on BrowseComp-Plus). — [arXiv 2606.13003](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.13003)
- **MASS, "Multi-Agent Design: Optimizing Agents with Better Prompts and Topologies"** (Zhou et al., Google/Cambridge, Feb 2025, arXiv 2502.02533; ICLR 2026). Three stages: block-level prompt optimization (MIPRO), then topology search pruned by each block's incremental influence, then workflow-level prompt optimization. — [arXiv 2502.02533](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2502.02533)
  - Gemini 1.5 Pro average: MASS **78.8%**, versus CoT 65.3%, Self-Consistency 68.2%, Self-Refine 66.9%, Multi-Agent Debate 70.3%, and ADAS 69.7%. Benchmarks: MATH, DROP, HotpotQA, MuSiQue, 2WikiMQA, MBPP, HumanEval, LiveCodeBench. — [arXiv 2502.02533](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2502.02533)
  - Ablation: block-level prompt optimization gave about **+6%** over single-agent prompt optimization, topology search another **~+3%**, and workflow-level prompt optimization another **~+2%**. Optimized prompts plus few-shot examples were **more token-effective than scaling agent count** with default prompts. Only a small fraction of topologies help. Task-specific winners: debate for multi-hop QA, aggregation for reasoning, reflection with tool use for coding. — [arXiv 2502.02533](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2502.02533)
  - Stated design principles: (1) optimize individual agents properly first; (2) compose only influential topologies; (3) optimize for interdependence at the workflow level. — [arXiv 2502.02533](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2502.02533)
- Activity in late 2026 is heavy but unconsolidated. Examples (titles and abstracts only): *Automated Multi-Agent Organization Design* (Oct 2026, arXiv 2610.27531, a survey from architecture search to organizational learning); *Mining the Design Space of Multi-Agent Workflows* (Dartmouth/AI2, Oct 2026, arXiv 2610.31709, which targets the field's "folk wisdom" about topologies); *WorkflowOps* (collaboration priors, so orchestrators are not memoryless, arXiv 2610.07860); *MoFlow* (multi-objective workflows covering accuracy, cost, latency, and robustness, arXiv 2609.38294); *FlowMAS* (GFlowNet topology learning, arXiv 2609.37151); *EMMA* (episodic memory for workflow synthesis, arXiv 2610.35966). — [2610.27531](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.27531), [2610.31709](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.31709), [2610.07860](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.07860), [2609.38294](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.38294), [2609.37151](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.37151), [2610.35966](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.35966)
- Benchmarks used to evaluate MAS in this literature: GAIA, AssistantBench, WebArena (Magentic-One); BrowseComp and BrowseComp-Plus (Anthropic; Illusion); SWE-Bench Lite, GPQA-Diamond, HLE (Illusion); MAST-Data with 1,642 annotated failure traces as a *diagnostic* resource; SMFR (588 items) as a MAS-suitable diagnostic task. — [arXiv 2503.13657](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2503.13657); [arXiv 2606.13003](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.13003); [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)
- Team size and topology scaling (other researcher's scope, mentioned briefly): Google's "Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems" (Kim et al., Dec 2025, arXiv 2512.08296) reports that centralized coordination helps parallelizable tasks but hurts sequential ones, and that independent agents amplify errors more than centralized ones. [abstract-level, not re-fetched] — [alphaXiv 2512.08296](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2512.08296). See also *The Ringelmann Effect in Multi-Agent LLM Systems* (May 2026, arXiv 2606.02646). — [alphaXiv 2606.02646](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.02646)

### Inferences
- In a harness, automated topology search is best treated as a **prompt and decomposition optimizer**, not a source of novel coordination. Searched designs should always be checked against a CoT-SC control at matched cost.

### Gaps
- The many Sept–Oct 2026 workflow-search papers have not been independently replicated. I read only their titles and abstracts.

## 5. Design-relevant findings: communication, shared context, roles, verification, overhead

### Takeaway
The evidence supports a small set of design rules. Prefer independent workers with isolated contexts and structured, low-bandwidth handoffs (artifacts or references, not full transcripts). Put the aggregation and verification logic in deterministic code or explicit checks. Keep coupled write tasks single-threaded. Spend role specialization on prompt and tool quality rather than on personas. Budget roughly 15× tokens, and justify that spend against a matched single-agent baseline.

### Cited Findings
- **Communication protocol and bandwidth.** Exchanging full solutions collapses diversity within a single round. Independent proposals plus synthesis (MoA-style) avoid this. The authors suggest lower-bandwidth signals instead: scores, method descriptions, failure causes. — [arXiv 2608.23541](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.23541). MetaGPT's structured documents and publish–subscribe pool reduced FC1 and FC2 failures relative to ChatDev's chat chains. — [arXiv 2503.13657](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2503.13657). MAST concludes that protocol fixes alone do not resolve misalignment (FC2 = 32.3%). — [arXiv 2503.13657](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2503.13657)
- **Shared memory and context.** Anthropic recommends external memory for plans and subagent outputs written to storage and passed back as references. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system). Cognition recommends sharing full traces and avoiding parallel writers. — [Biweekly Engineering](https://biweekly-engineering.beehiiv.com/p/the-case-against-multi-agents-biweekly-engineering-episode-42). MAST's context-loss modes (loss of history 2.8%, conversation reset 2.2%, information withholding 0.8%, the last being "fatal") are rare but serious. — [arXiv 2503.13657](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2503.13657)
- **Role specialization.** Role personas add little. DyLAN's role agents converged unanimously and an "all-assistant" setup beat the specialists, and ADAS's specialized-coordination motifs added negligible gain. — [arXiv 2606.13003](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.13003). Role disobedience is only 1.5% of MAST failures. — [arXiv 2503.13657](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2503.13657). What does help is per-agent prompt optimization (MASS +6%). — [arXiv 2502.02533](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2502.02533). Specialization that uses different tools, contexts, or deterministic code helps a lot: Expert-MAS took GPT-5 from 57.0% to 96.5% on SMFR. — [arXiv 2606.13003](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.13003). Model diversity, as opposed to persona diversity, gives a measurable gain (+0.188) if outputs stay independent. — [arXiv 2608.23541](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.23541)
- **Verification agents.** FC3 is 23.5% of failures and affects strong models too. Low-level, end-stage checks alone are insufficient, so verify at multiple levels using external knowledge and tests. — [arXiv 2503.13657](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2503.13657). LLM judges and verifiers show positional bias (MAS-Zero >45% early-position preference) — [arXiv 2606.13003](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.13003) — and judge mistakes plus wrong-answer propagation. — [arXiv 2402.18272](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2402.18272). Critique helps only on locatable faults and degraded graded solutions in 57% of first rounds. — [arXiv 2608.23541](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.23541). ChatDev's explicit test/review phases cut FC3 relative to MetaGPT. — [arXiv 2503.13657](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2503.13657)
- **Debate dynamics.** Consensus can be steered by "agreement intensity" (about +15% on MedQA when tuned), but the optimum depends on the dataset. Contrarian "devil" roles can hurt. — [arXiv 2311.17371](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2311.17371). Agreement does not imply correct reasoning. — [arXiv 2606.08457](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.08457)
- **Termination and loop control.** Step repetition (15.7%) and unawareness of termination conditions (12.4%, "fatal") are the top two MAST modes, ahead of any reasoning failure. — [arXiv 2503.13657](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2503.13657). Anthropic observed endless searching and 50-subagent over-spawning, and addressed them with effort-scaling rules in the prompts. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system). Magentic-One's Progress Ledger explicitly asks each turn whether the task is complete and whether progress is being made, and replans on stalls. [abstract-level] — [alphaXiv 2411.04468](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2411.04468)
- **Orchestration overhead and cost.** Multi-agent costs about 15× chat tokens. A synchronous orchestrator blocks on its slowest worker. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system). Automatic MAS cost more than 10× CoT-SC for equal or worse accuracy. — [arXiv 2606.13003](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.13003). MAD gains are sensitive to hyperparameters and do not transfer across models (GPT-4 yes, Mixtral no). — [arXiv 2311.17371](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2311.17371)

### Inferences (design checklist for a harness)
1. **Default to a single agent with self-consistency or best-of-N.** Add multi-agent structure only where the task is parallelizable or read-heavy, or exceeds one context window. Measure every addition against the matched-token baseline.
2. **Orchestrator-worker with deterministic glue.** The lead plans and delegates with explicit objectives, output formats, and boundaries. Code handles fan-out, joins, voting, and comparisons where possible, which is the recipe behind Expert-MAS.
3. **Isolate worker contexts and keep proposals independent.** Return compressed findings or artifact references. Avoid peers reading each other's full drafts.
4. **One writer for coupled artifacts.** Parallel subagents investigate. A single agent applies changes to code or documents.
5. **Use concrete verification, not just more opinions.** Tests, executable checks, and external sources at several stages. Critique should target locatable faults. Watch judges for position and conformity bias.
6. **Build explicit termination and progress tracking** (ledger-style), plus effort-scaling heuristics, to address MAST's top modes (repetition and termination).
7. **Instrument with MAST categories** (the released LLM annotator and MAST-Data) to diagnose where a design fails. Tracing, checkpoint/resume, and end-state LLM-judge evals follow Anthropic's practice.

### Gaps
- I found no controlled study isolating *asynchronous* versus *synchronous* orchestration in LLM MAS; Anthropic lists async only as future work.
- Head-to-head evidence on shared-memory designs (blackboard vs message passing vs artifact references) under matched budgets is thin. Most evidence is architectural comparison (MetaGPT vs ChatDev) or practitioner reports.
